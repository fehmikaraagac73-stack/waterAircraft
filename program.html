Program & status · Updated June 2026

Where the build actually stands.

This page is written for investors, and the useful version of it is the one that says what has not happened yet as clearly as what has.

Status as of June 2026: Phase 1, design study. No airframe has been acquired. No hull has been laid. No classification society has been appointed and no plans have been submitted for approval. No sea trials, tank tests or inclining experiments have been conducted. Every figure published on this site is a design target derived from general arrangement studies.

Program

Three phases, gated.

Each phase is gated on the previous one completing — Phase 2 begins when Phase 1 has produced a vessel that floats, moves and satisfies a surveyor.

PHASE 01 · CURRENT

Research & develop prototype

Build one demonstration vessel. The goal is not a saleable ship but evidence: measured flotation, propulsion, stability and customer experience. The test schedule is below.

The airframe is the cheap part. The value sits in the engineering and the certification route that turns one into a registered vessel, and so does the risk.

Risk register

The things most likely to go wrong.

Certification risk

A first-of-type has no precedent for a society to reason from. Approval may require an equivalent-safety case, which takes longer and costs more than a prescriptive route. This is the largest single risk in the program.

Weight growth

Marine outfit is heavier than aviation outfit for the same volume. If lightship weight grows, draft grows and speed falls. The inclining experiment is the first honest measurement of this.

Corrosion over service life

Aluminium superstructures are conventional, but a fuselage has far more faying surfaces and fasteners than a purpose-built deckhouse. Service-life cost depends on the detailing.

Berth compatibility

A 22.4 m beam rules out a meaningful number of berths and locks. The addressable route map is narrower than the length overall suggests, and needs surveying per customer.

Yard capability

Few yards have both aluminium aerostructure experience and marine hull experience. The conversion line in Phase 2 exists partly because subcontracting this is difficult.

Market depth

Novelty is expected to sell the first hull. Whether it sells the tenth is unproven. The cargo configuration is the one with a business case independent of novelty.
