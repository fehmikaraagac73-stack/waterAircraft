About ANUNNAKI

Aviation engineering meets marine technology.

An aircraft-to-marine conversion platform, creating an entirely new category of vessel by transforming retired aircraft into passenger boats, cargo vessels, luxury yachts and specialised marine platforms.

The idea

Two industries that have never had a reason to talk to each other.

Aviation retires roughly 500 to 700 commercial aircraft a year. Almost all of them are parted out for the components with resale value and the remaining structure is scrapped. Meanwhile, shipbuilding fabricates aluminium superstructures from raw plate, at considerable cost, to sit on top of hulls.

Nobody had a strong reason to connect those two facts, because the industries have different regulators, different suppliers, different certification bodies and almost no overlapping personnel. ANUNNAKI exists to sit in that gap: aviation structures on the top, marine engineering underneath, and a certification route that satisfies the marine side because the marine side is the one that governs.

The output is a category that did not previously exist — vessels that are recognisable from three miles away, sustainable in a way that is checkable rather than asserted, and high-value for governments, tourism operators, commercial customers and private owners.

Scope

What we do, and what we do not.

We do

  • Airframe selection, survey and acquisition
  • Structural preparation, corrosion treatment and reinforcement
  • Naval architecture for the pontoon hull and deck structure
  • Marine integration — propulsion, machinery, tankage, systems
  • Interior arrangement and fit-out specification
  • Managing the class approval and flag registration route
  • Sea trials, handover and crew familiarisation

We do not

  • Operate vessels — the customer or their operator does
  • Certify our own vessels — a recognised society does
  • Build aircraft, or return any airframe to flight
  • Sell parts recovered from the airframes we convert
  • Promise a delivery date before an airframe is secured
  • Publish performance figures we have not measured

On the name

Yes, we know what the search results look like.

"Anunnaki" is a Sumerian term for a group of deities, and in the last few decades it has been thoroughly adopted by ancient-astronaut literature. Searching the name returns that material long before it returns anything about vessels.

The name was chosen for the older meaning — something between the sky and the water, which is a reasonable description of the product. We are aware it brings the other associations along with it, and the response is not to hide from that but to make every page of this site more technical than it strictly needs to be. A general arrangement drawing and a damage stability discussion answer the question better than a rebrand would.

If a customer's procurement process finds the association a problem, we would rather have that conversation early. It is a marketing question with a real answer, not something to be managed with better photography.

On the imagery

Why this site is drawings rather than renders.

Earlier ANUNNAKI material used generated vessel renders. Presented small, they read well. Presented at full width on a website, their artefacts become obvious — garbled lettering, a logo that changes between images, and in one case a yacht depicted as a business jet when the specification describes a 747.

The audience most likely to notice those problems — naval architects, class surveyors, port authorities, procurement teams — is precisely the audience this site has to convince. So the site leads with general arrangement drawings, which are honest about what they represent, and treats photography as something to be added once the Phase 1 vessel exists to photograph.

Clean renders produced from the arrangement drawings will be commissioned before any public launch. Until then, lines and dimensions.
