About ANUNNAKI

Aviation engineering and marine technology in one vessel.

An aircraft-to-marine conversion platform, creating an entirely new category of vessel by transforming retired aircraft into passenger boats, cargo vessels, luxury yachts and specialised marine platforms.

The idea

Two industries with no established overlap.

Aviation retires roughly 500 to 700 commercial aircraft a year. Almost all of them are parted out for the components with resale value and the remaining structure is scrapped. Meanwhile, shipbuilding fabricates aluminium superstructures from raw plate, at considerable cost, to sit on top of hulls.

The two industries have different regulators, suppliers and certification bodies, and almost no overlapping personnel, so nobody had reason to connect those facts. ANUNNAKI sits in that gap: aviation structures above, marine engineering below, and a certification route run to marine rules, because those are the ones that govern.

The output is a category that did not previously exist — vessels with a distinctive profile, a reuse benefit that is checkable rather than asserted, and application across governments, tourism operators, commercial customers and private owners.

Scope

What we do, and what we do not.

We do

  • Airframe selection, survey and acquisition
  • Structural preparation, corrosion treatment and reinforcement
  • Naval architecture for the pontoon hull and deck structure
  • Marine integration — propulsion, machinery, tankage, systems
  • Interior arrangement and fit-out specification
  • Managing the class approval and flag registration route
  • Sea trials, handover and crew familiarisation

We do not

  • Operate vessels — the customer or their operator does
  • Certify our own vessels — a recognised society does
  • Build aircraft, or return any airframe to flight
  • Sell parts recovered from the airframes we convert
  • Promise a delivery date before an airframe is secured
  • Publish performance figures we have not measured

On the name

The name and what it returns in a search.

"Anunnaki" is a Sumerian term for a group of deities, and in the last few decades it has been thoroughly adopted by ancient-astronaut literature. Searching the name returns that material long before it returns anything about vessels.

The name was chosen for the older meaning, which refers to both sky and water and so describes the product accurately. The response to the other associations is to make the site more technical rather than to rebrand. If a customer's procurement process finds the association a problem, we would rather have that conversation early.

On the imagery

Why this site is drawings rather than renders.

Earlier ANUNNAKI material used generated vessel renders. At full width their artefacts become obvious — garbled lettering, a logo that changes between images, and in one case a yacht depicted as a business jet when the specification describes a 747.

The audience most likely to notice — naval architects, class surveyors, port authorities, procurement teams — is the audience this site has to convince, so it leads with general arrangement drawings instead. Clean renders will be commissioned from those drawings before any public launch.
