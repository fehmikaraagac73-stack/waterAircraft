General Cargo Watercraft

747 Cargo Barge. Up to 120 tonnes of payload in roughly 1,500 m² of enclosed, weather-tight cargo volume, using the freighter's original side cargo doors and reinforced floor structure.

SOLAS USCG 46 CFR SUBCH. T MODULAR LASHING 5 T DAVIT — OPTIONAL

Description

The configuration with the lowest conversion cost.

A 747 freighter already has most of what a cargo vessel needs: the cargo doors, the reinforced floor and the load paths. What it lacks is buoyancy.

Starting from a 747-400F rather than a passenger airframe makes the conversion substantially cheaper. The freighter already carries a reinforced main deck floor beam structure rated for palletised loads, a large side cargo door, roller-bed and restraint provisions at fixed pitch, and a nine-G rigid barrier behind the flight deck — all expensive to add to a hull, and inherited free.

What we add is the marine half. The fuselage is sealed and subdivided, an aft access ramp is cut into the rear pressure bulkhead station and hinged to the deck, and the original side door is re-hung with marine seals so it can be worked alongside. Modular lashing rails on the deck grid let the same vessel handle palletised freight, containers up to a limited stack, and project cargo. A five-tonne foredeck davit is offered for berths without shore cranes.

Use cases

Applications for this configuration.

Island and archipelago supply

Scheduled resupply to communities with a quay but no cargo handling infrastructure. The aft ramp and davit make the vessel self-sufficient at the receiving end.

Project and out-of-gauge cargo

A 6.1 m wide, 3 m tall clear internal envelope running most of the vessel's length takes loads that a standard container flow cannot.

Disaster relief and logistics

Enclosed, weather-tight volume with independent power and ramp discharge into a damaged port. Relevant to government and NGO procurement rather than commercial freight rates.

Short-haul regional freight

Coastal and inter-island routes at 12–16 kn where transit time is measured in hours and the vessel returns to the same two berths repeatedly.

Key features

Cargo configuration.

01High payload capacity

Up to 120 tonnes distributed across three bays and the open working deck.

02Wide cargo doors

The freighter's original side door retained at 5.6 m clear width, re-hung for marine service.

03Modular container lashing

A deck grid that accepts palletised freight, limited container stacks and project cargo alike.

04Heavy-duty deck structure

Transverse girders sized for point loads rather than distributed accommodation weight.

05Bulkheads & compartments

Watertight subdivision through both the fuselage bays and the pontoon hulls.

06GPS tracking system

Vessel and consignment tracking for operators running scheduled supply routes.

07Stable pontoon hull

Wide separation keeps the platform level during ramp operations and crane lifts alongside.

08SOLAS / Subchapter T

Same compliance target as the passenger vessel, with cargo securing to the applicable code.
