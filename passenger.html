Passenger Watercraft

747 Cruise Liner. Up to 450 passengers across a complete Boeing 747 fuselage mounted on a twin pontoon hull, with the upper deck retained as a panoramic observation lounge.

SOLAS USCG 46 CFR SUBCH. T WHEELCHAIR ACCESSIBLE 8 × 50-PERSON RAFTS

Description

A ferry that people will queue for a photograph of before they board.

The commercial argument is not novelty. It is that a route with a memorable vessel on it carries a fare premium and generates its own marketing.

The passenger configuration is the most demanding of the three and the one that sets the engineering standard for the range. Four hundred and fifty people aboard a hull form with no precedent means the subdivision, egress and stability case has to be unambiguous before anything else is discussed.

Internally the fuselage is treated as a single long superstructure divided into four fire zones. The forward section under the 747's upper deck becomes the grand lounge, with the original overhead volume opened up. Amidships is the dining area and galley, served from a service passage down the port side. Aft of the wing box station are the VIP suites and, at the very rear, crew accommodation and the machinery access trunk down into the starboard pontoon.

The upper deck — the 747's signature hump — becomes the panoramic observation deck. It is the single most valuable square metre on the vessel and it exists because of the airframe we started with, not despite it.

Boarding is at main deck level from either side, with the deck sitting close to typical quay height so that a level or near-level gangway is achievable at most berths. Step-free circulation runs the full length of the main deck; the observation deck is reached by both stair and lift.

Use cases

Where this configuration earns.

Island-hopping ferry routes

Short protected-water crossings where the journey is part of the holiday. The vessel converts a transfer leg into an attraction, which is the strongest single argument for a tourism-board buyer.

Day-cruise and excursion operators

Four to eight hour circuits with catering, an observation deck and a fixed return berth. High passenger turnover, no overnight accommodation obligations.

Resort shuttle and floating venue

Alongside at a resort as an event and dining venue, running as a working vessel on scheduled transfers. Two revenue modes from one asset.

Government tourism programmes

Where a national tourism authority is funding a landmark asset, the airframe reuse story and the emissions profile of the hybrid option both carry weight in grant assessment.

Key features

Passenger configuration.

01Stable pontoon hull

Wide-separation twin hulls for a large righting arm and a comfortable platform alongside.

02Twin diesel-electric

2 × 2,500 kW with azimuth pods for manoeuvring in confined harbours without tug assistance.

03Fire detection & sprinkler

Full detection coverage and sprinkler protection in all accommodation and service spaces.

04Advanced navigation

Radar, AIS, ECDIS and GPS tracking, mast carried on the retained vertical stabiliser.

05Life rafts both sides

Eight 50-person rafts, four per side, with muster stations on the open promenade deck.

06Wheelchair accessible

Step-free circulation the full length of the main deck, lift to the observation deck.

07Panoramic windows

Original window stations enlarged where frame spacing allows; continuous glazed band on the upper deck.

08SOLAS / Subchapter T

Designed against both from the outset rather than retrofitted to fit after the fact.
