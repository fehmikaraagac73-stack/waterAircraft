Three advanced watercraft designs

One Boeing 747 airframe. Three vessels.

Single 747 fuselage. Wings removed, tail assembly retained. Built to comply with IMO, SOLAS and USCG 46 CFR Subchapter T. What differs between the three is deck arrangement, propulsion sizing, and the annex each is registered under.

Common to all three

Design advantages carried across the range.

01 Complete fuselage, wings removed

One 747 fuselage used entire, with the tail assembly retained for recognisability and as a mounting point for navigation and communications equipment.

02 High-strength aluminium structure

Aerospace alloy skin and frames, corrosion-treated and reinforced at the marine deck interface where load transfers into the hull structure.

03 Twin pontoon hulls

Displacement, machinery and tankage live in the pontoons. Wide separation gives a large righting-moment arm and a stable platform at rest.

04 Watertight compartmentalisation

Bulkheads at original frame stations subdivide both the fuselage and the pontoons, so a single compartment flooding does not threaten the vessel.

05 Fire detection & suppression

Smoke detection, sprinkler coverage in accommodation spaces, and marked emergency exits on both sides throughout the length.

06 Advanced navigation & monitoring

Radar, AIS, ECDIS, GPS tracking and machinery monitoring, with the mast arrangement carried on the retained vertical stabiliser.

07 Hybrid and electric options

Four propulsion packages across the range — twin diesel, diesel-electric, hybrid, and full electric for short protected-water routes.

08 Built for registration

Designed from the outset against SOLAS, IMO codes and 46 CFR Subchapter T so the vessel has a plausible flag and class route.
