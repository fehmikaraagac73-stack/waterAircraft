The proposition

Aviation structures are lighter, stronger and better documented than anything the boatyard has.

A 747 fuselage is a pressure vessel built to survive 90,000 flight cycles. It arrives with complete structural documentation, known alloy specifications, and a frame-and-stringer geometry that a naval architect can analyse rather than guess at. What it lacks is buoyancy, a propulsion train, and a regulatory home.

We supply those three things. The fuselage is stripped, corrosion-treated and sealed; watertight bulkheads are installed at the original frame stations; the whole assembly is mounted on a twin pontoon hull that carries the displacement, the machinery and the stability. The airframe becomes superstructure — which is exactly the load case it is good at.

Process

How a conversion works

Four stages between an airframe on a desert storage line and a vessel with a flag.

Airframe selection

Sourcing from storage and part-out inventory. Corrosion survey, structural repair history, and frame-station integrity decide whether a hull is a candidate. Wings, engines and landing gear are removed and returned to the parts market.

Structural preparation

Interior strip, corrosion treatment, and installation of watertight bulkheads at the original frame stations. Marine-grade reinforcement is added at the cradle interface where the fuselage transfers load into the deck structure.

Marine integration

Twin pontoon hulls, deck structure, machinery spaces, tankage and propulsion. This is where the vessel gets its displacement, its stability curve and its class case — the fuselage above is superstructure from here on.

Certification & delivery

Inclining experiment, sea trials, class survey, flag registration, and crew familiarisation. Delivered against SOLAS, applicable IMO codes and — for US operation — 46 CFR Subchapter T.

Compliance & safety

The regulatory case is the product.

A novel hull form does not get a novel rulebook. Every design decision here was made to keep the vessel inside frameworks that already exist.

SOLAS

Safety of Life at Sea

Subdivision and damage stability, fire protection with detection and sprinkler coverage, life-saving appliances, and means of escape. Eight 50-person life rafts on the passenger configuration, mounted both sides.

46 CFR SUBCH. T

USCG small passenger vessels

The route for US-flag passenger operation. Governs stability, watertight integrity, egress arrangement, and crew requirements for the vessels in this size and service class.

IMO CODES

International instruments

Load line, MARPOL for discharge and emissions, COLREG for navigation lights and shapes, and the applicable intact stability code. The yacht configuration additionally targets the Large Yacht Code.

CLASS & FLAG

Society and registration path

Plan approval, survey during construction, and class notation from a recognised society, followed by flag registration. This is the step that turns an unusual object into a ship.

Program

Three phases. We are in the first one.

PHASE 01

Research & prototype

Build one demonstration vessel to prove four things:

  • Flotation — displacement, trim and freeboard against calculation
  • Propulsion — thrust, manoeuvring and station-keeping
  • Stability — inclining experiment and intact stability curve
  • Customer experience — boarding, circulation, comfort at speed
